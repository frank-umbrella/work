Read this first

Loadout is a web app for the office and the warehouse, used on a phone in the rack room and on a desktop at the front. It turns an approved Portal proposal into a pick ticket and follows every part on it from ordered to installed. It keeps a running count of what is on the shelves, including how many feet are left in each wire box. Every change is written to a log with the name of the person who made it, so any count can be explained later. It is built and in use; this document now describes the app as it is, and the open questions that remain are marked.

Every client, name, number, price, and address on this page is invented sample data. The buttons drawn inside the pictures are pictures; the only things you can click on this page are the four tabs above, the contents links below, and the print buttons at the bottom.

Overview

The short version of how Loadout works, written to be read in five minutes. The Storyboard tab shows the screens; the SOP tab has the step-by-step procedures. Print this tab on its own with the button at the bottom.

The problem it solves

A proposal is approved in Portal, product gets bought, and boxes land at the shop. Before Loadout it was memory and sticky notes from there: which boxes belong to which job, what is on the shelf, how many feet are left in a wire box, and who has the spare access point in their van. Loadout puts one record behind each of those questions, and a scan with a phone is how you answer them.

How a job moves through it

  1. The proposal is accepted in Portal. Download its CSV. In Loadout open Tickets, press New ticket, keep Import a Portal CSV, and press Create ticket. Loadout builds pick ticket #1042 with every part grouped by area. A job with no proposal can use Build by hand instead.
  2. Pull what we already have. On the ticket, Pull everything in stock fills what the shelves can cover. On the phone, scan the shelf label and the product, press Pull for ticket, and Pull; the parts go into the job's staging bay. Show on board puts the ticket on the TV by the bays as the pick path.
  3. Order the rest. Order missing puts every line marked Short on a draft purchase order. Pick the vendor, press Mark ordered with the vendor's order number, and say whether it ships to the office or straight to the job site. When the ship notice arrives, Add shipment takes the tracking number.
  4. Watch it come in. Tracking shows packages coming to the office on the left and packages going straight to clients on the right, each with its status and the time of that status. A box delivered here but never scanned sits on top in amber and reads Delivered, not received. Week lays out the install days with each ticket's lines and where they stand.
  5. Receive it. Press Receive on the package's card. Lines for a ticket go into its staging bay; anything at or above $250 gets its own QR and Print labels comes up on the spot. A box shipped to a job site gets Mark delivered instead and never touches the shelves.
  6. Wire. Each box of cable has a label. After pulling cable, scan it, type the footage mark into Mark now, and press Save cut. Loadout logs the cut with your name and the ticket, and the box's feet left update.
  7. Deliver. On install day press Deliver to site on each line going out. The serialized units on it check out to whoever delivers. Anything that comes back is scanned and checked in with its condition.
  8. Close. Close ticket asks what to do with each leftover: Return to stock or Write off with a reason. Units still out become Installed at the job. Copy for Portal copies a status note to paste into the proposal.

What it keeps track of

  • Products and where they sit. Every product has a shelf, a count, an optional photo, and a barcode learned the first time it is scanned.
  • Wire boxes. Feet left in every box, and every cut: how much, by whom, for which job.
  • Serialized units. Anything at or above the QR threshold gets its own label, named like "Ubiquiti UDM-SE #1". One scan shows where it is, who has it, and its history; a service call years later can scan the label in the rack and see when it went in.
  • Pick tickets. One per approved proposal, with its install date: needed, from stock, to order, staged in the bay, delivered.
  • Orders and shipments. Vendor, order number, where it ships, carrier, tracking number, expected date, and every status change with who made it and when.
  • A log of every change. Every movement (receive, pull, move, stage, deliver, cut, reading, adjust, checkout, checkin, return, writeoff, edit) carries a name and a time. Nothing changes anonymously.

The screens

  • Tickets: the pick tickets by status, with filters and an Install column; open one to see every line by area and its buttons.
  • Scan: the camera and whatever the scan found: a product card, a unit card, a wire box's box sheet, or a shelf.
  • Stock: the catalog with All, Consumables (wire boxes with feet left), Low stock, and Locations. A row opens in place with its buttons.
  • Orders: purchase orders, their lines, and their shipments.
  • Tracking: every package on its way, office on the left, clients on the right, recently delivered below.
  • Week: the install schedule as a timesheet, one block per day, with each ticket's lines and their status.
  • Items: every serialized unit, with Out as the Friday list of who has what.
  • Console (admin and accounting): Reports with export, People, Settings, About (the data dictionary), and Export everything.
  • More: the movement log, clients and jobs, labels, the inventory sheet importer, and the Add to home screen card. On the phone the tab bar is Tickets, Scan, Week, Stock, More, and the rest sit at the top of More.

The wall board is a tenth screen with no menu: the TV by the bays, read-only and live.

Portal

Loadout works with Portal through the proposal CSV that Portal already exports, on any Portal plan, and Copy for Portal hands a status note back to paste in. On the Premium plan, Zapier can push an accepted proposal into Loadout on its own: the worker that receives it is built, and a ticket it makes carries an "Imported from Portal, review" chip until someone presses Reviewed. Portal's full API, which would let Loadout write its note onto the proposal by itself, is only on the Unlimited plan after an application to Portal.

What we need from you

The app runs on assumed defaults for these. Each answer is a small change:

  1. Which Portal plan the company is on, so we know whether the Zapier import and automatic notes can be turned on.
  2. Who is in each role: admin, warehouse, tech, accounting; and whether techs may receive product.
  3. One real proposal CSV from Portal, so the import is checked against the real export.
  4. Wire box start lengths: keep the defaults by cable type (1,000 ft for Cat5e, Cat6, and coax; 500 ft for audio, speaker, and stranded) or type each open box's start once.
  5. Assigned inventory: whether there is a per-job list beside the Unassigned Inventory sheet, and whether it should come in as staged stock.
  6. What will drive the TV by the bays: a small PC, a streaming stick, or a laptop.
  7. Turn on Firebase Storage for the project and deploy the storage rules, so product photos can be saved.
  8. A service account for the worker with two roles, Cloud Datastore User and Storage Object Admin, so the nightly backup and the Zapier import can go live. No Drive folder is needed.

What it is not

Not a replacement for Portal or for QuickBooks, no automatic carrier tracking yet, and no unit-by-unit tracking of cheap stock unless you ask for it. The full list is at the end of the SOP tab.

Part 1.A day in the warehouse

One job, start to finish: Acme Dental's main office, pick ticket #1042. Every panel is drawn from the app as built in release 0.5.2: the same top bar, buttons, and status words. Phone screens are what a person holds in the warehouse or the van; desktop screens are the office. Panels 14 and 15 are the two planning screens the office reads each morning.

1Approved proposal arrives

What you doDownload the accepted proposal's CSV from Portal. In Loadout open Tickets, press New ticket, keep Import a Portal CSV, and press Choose a CSV file. Check the client, job, proposal number, staging bay, and install date, read the Skipped rows list, and press Create ticket.

What Loadout recordsNo stock moves yet: pick ticket #1042 for Acme Dental, Main office, proposal 2291, marked Imported from CSV, with who created it and when, and every part grouped by area.

2Pull from stock

What you doAt Rack A3, open Scan and scan the shelf label so the location chip reads Rack A3, then scan the U6-Pro box. On the product card press Pull for ticket, choose #1042, and press Continue. In Pull from stock, check From Rack A3 and Quantity 2, press Pull, and set the two APs in Bay 2.

What Loadout recordsA pull movement: who, Ubiquiti U6-Pro x2, from Rack A3 into Bay 2, for ticket #1042. Rack A3 is now empty, so the line reads Short 2 in red: 2 staged, and 2 that no shelf or PO covers yet.

3Order the rest

What you doOn the ticket, press Pull everything in stock first; what the shelves cannot cover reads Short, in red. Then press Order missing, and the three uncovered lines go onto draft PO 7731. Open it under Orders, pick ADI in Vendor, and when the order is placed press Mark ordered, type ADI's order number, and say it ships to the office.

What Loadout recordsNo stock moves: PO 7731 to ADI with each line tied to its ticket line and its unit cost on the day it was ordered, and who marked it ordered and when. Each of those lines turns On order, in amber, with a chip such as PO 7731: Ordered, Oct 27.

4Add the shipment

What you doWhen ADI's ship notice arrives, open PO 7731 under Orders and press Add shipment. Paste the tracking number (Loadout reads 1Z as UPS), tick the lines in that box, and type the expected date. When ADI says the U6-Pros are backordered, set that line to Backordered with the vendor's ETA.

What Loadout recordsNo stock moves: a shipment on PO 7731 with carrier, tracking number, the lines it carries, and a status log that starts at Shipped with who added it and when. From here the package shows on Tracking (panel 15).

5Receive

What you doThe box Tracking flagged is on the bench. On the phone open More, Tracking, and press Receive on its card (or open PO 7731 under Orders). Scan each UPC or keep the quantity shown, check that both lines go into Bay 2, and press Receive. Both units cost at least $250, so Loadout offers Print 2 labels; print them and stick them on before the units leave the bench.

What Loadout recordsA receive movement per line: who, product, quantity, PO 7731 and its shipment, into Bay 2, counted as staged on #1042. Two serialized units, Ubiquiti UDM-SE #1 and Ubiquiti USW-Pro-24-PoE #1. The shipment's status log adds Received.

6Label

What you doOpen More, Labels. Pick the sheet (2 x 4 for bays and boxes, Avery 5160 for small shelf labels), tick the bay, the wire box, and the unit, and press Print. Stick each label where it gets scanned: the bay upright, the box beside the cable exit, the unit itself.

What Loadout recordsNo movement: each label carries the record's code and a readable line under the QR, so a torn one can still be typed into Scan.

7Wire box

What you doAfter pulling cable, read the footage printed where the cable leaves the box and scan the box's label. On the box sheet type 640 into Mark now (or step down with -50, -100, -250). Cut this time fills in as 360. Check For ticket says #1042 (it fills in on its own when the box sits in the job's bay or you scanned that bay last) and press Save cut.

What Loadout recordsA cut movement: who, Cat6 blue box 3, from mark 1,000 to 640, 360 ft, for ticket #1042, where the line's staged feet go up by 360. The box now reads 640 ft left.

8Load the van

What you doWith Bay 2 loaded into the van, open #1042 and press Deliver to site on each line you are taking today. Each press takes what is staged on that line to site, and the serialized units on it check out to you in the same tap.

What Loadout recordsA deliver movement per line (who, product, quantity, ticket, job) and a checkout movement per serialized unit with you as the holder. The ticket turns Delivered once every line is on site.

9On site, years later

What you doOn a service call, scan the label on the unit in the rack with your phone, or type the ID under it into Scan. At the office, open Items, Installed, and the row opens in place. It shows the unit went in on 11/06/2026 under proposal 2291, ticket #1042.

What Loadout recordsNothing new: the unit's history already holds the receive movement, the checkout from Deliver to site, and the adjust that marked it Installed at close, each with a name and a date.

10Something comes back

What you doBack at the shop with an AP that was not needed, scan its label. The unit card shows it out with you; press Check in, confirm Back to (Rack A3, its usual shelf), pick the condition, and press Check in.

What Loadout recordsA checkin movement: who, Ubiquiti U6-Pro #4, back from ticket #1042, into Rack A3, condition Good.

11End of day

What you doBefore a wire box goes back on the rack or into the van, scan it and save the mark. In a dead corner of the warehouse it still saves on the phone, and the pill at the top reads Offline or 2 pending until there is signal. Before you leave, check that it says Synced.

What Loadout recordsA cut movement per box (who, box, marks, feet, ticket). Synced means every change from the day reached the server; More, Movements lists them, with Previous, This week, and Next to page through.

12Accounting

What you doOpen Console, Reports, pick Delivered, leave This month, and press Export XLSX. The same row of reports has Stock on hand, Spend, Items out, Wire usage, Movements, and Aging, each with a From and To date and a total line.

What Loadout recordsNothing: reports read the movement log for the range, and the accounting role sees no buttons that change anything, so a count cannot move from here. Cost is the unit cost on the PO line, fixed the day it was ordered.

13The wall board

What you doNothing on the TV. On the ticket, press Show on board. The screen by the bays shows what is left to pull, grouped by the shelf it sits on in rack order, greys each line as the phone scans it into the bay and marks it Staged with a check, shows On order lines in amber with their PO and ETA, and Short lines in red. If the connection drops, a red Not live since strip says so.

What Loadout recordsNothing: the board signs in as the Board role, which reads everything and changes nothing, so a stray hand on the screen cannot move a count.

14The Week view

What you doOpen Week each morning. Every ticket sits on the day of its install date, with its lines and a status on the right: Pull, Staged ("Staged 2 of 4" when part of it is in the bay), On order with the ETA, Short, or Delivered. Press a card to open the ticket, or a line to open that line on it. Work week hides Saturday and Sunday; tickets with no install date wait in the Unscheduled list below the grid.

What Loadout recordsNothing: the view reads the tickets and their lines. Setting an install date, on the ticket or in the Unscheduled list, is the only change made here, and Work week is remembered for you on every device.

15Tracking

What you doOpen Tracking each morning. Packages coming to the office are on the left; packages a vendor ships straight to a job site are on the right, grouped by client and job. Each card shows its status with the date and time. Receive the box that was delivered here but never scanned (it sits on top in amber). For a drop-shipped box, press Mark delivered once the site has it. Late packages say how late, in amber.

What Loadout recordsNothing until you act. Receive writes receive movements (panel 5). Mark delivered counts the lines as received on the PO and staged on the ticket without adding anything to the shelves, and the card reads Delivered to site, with your name and the time in the shipment's status log.

Part 2.Functional SOP

Twelve procedures, in the order product moves. Button and screen names match the app as built (0.5.2) and the Storyboard tab. "Recorded" is what lands in the movement log, which is the audit trail behind every count.

SOP-1Receiving product

Get every box that arrives into Loadout the day it lands, against its PO when there is one.

WhoWarehouse or adminWhenThe day a delivery arrives, before the box is opened or shelved

With a PO

  1. Open Tracking. A box delivered to the office and not scanned in reads Delivered, not received and sits at the top of the Office column in amber. Press Receive on its card, or open the PO under Orders and press Receive on the shipment.
  2. The Receive shipment window lists each line in the box with its quantity, a chip for the ticket it serves (or Stock), and Gets its own QR when the unit costs at or above the QR threshold.
  3. Scan each box's UPC in Scan a UPC to count it, or keep the quantity shown. A line of wire asks for a Box label: the code written on the box.
  4. Check where each line goes: Into the bay for a ticket line, Onto a shelf for stock. Press Receive.
  5. When units got their own QR, Print labels comes up. Print them and stick each one on its unit before it leaves the bench.
  6. If the shipment is short, type what arrived. The rest stays open on the PO and on Tracking until it comes.
  7. A box the vendor shipped straight to a job site never comes through here: press Mark delivered on its card in the To clients column instead (SOP-5).

Without a PO

  1. Open Scan and scan the box's UPC. If Loadout does not know it, pick the product under Link to a product without a UPC, or press New product.
  2. On the product card press Receive (Receive a box for wire), type the quantity, and pick where it goes.
  3. To follow a cheaper product unit by unit anyway (for example every AP), open it under Stock and press Serialize; from then on every unit gets its own QR at receive.

Settled: the QR threshold is $250 by default; an admin changes it in Console, Settings.

Recorded: a receive movement with who, product, quantity, PO and shipment, the bay or shelf it went to, and the ticket when the line serves one; a unit record per unit at or above the threshold, named like "Ubiquiti UDM-SE #1"; and Received in the shipment's status log.

SOP-2Labeling

Put a QR label on every place and every followed thing, so one scan answers where it is and who has it.

WhoWarehouseWhenA new shelf, bay, or van is set up; a wire box or serialized unit is received; a label is torn or will not scan

  1. Open More, Labels (Stock's Print labels button goes to the same place).
  2. Pick the Sheet: 2 x 4 in, 10 per sheet for bays, racks, and wire boxes; Avery 5160, 30 per sheet for shelf edges and small parts.
  3. Tick what to print: locations (shelves, bays, vans), wire boxes, or units. Select all and Clear help with long lists; Include empty boxes adds boxes that ran out.
  4. Press Print. Check the sheet preview, press Print in the preview bar, and print on the label stock. Close preview goes back.
  5. Stick bay labels on the bay upright at eye height, shelf labels on the shelf edge, wire box labels on the side next to the cable exit, and unit labels on the unit itself, not only the carton, so the label goes into the rack with it.
  6. Scan each new label once on Scan to check it reads.
  7. To re-label one thing, open it (its row under Stock or Items, or its box sheet) and press Print label. If the old label is torn, type the readable line under the QR into Scan's code field and press Look up to find the record.

Recorded: no movement. The label carries the record's code and a readable line, so a torn label can always be typed in.

SOP-3Building a pick ticket from an approved proposal

Turn an accepted Portal proposal into the one working document for that job.

WhoWarehouse or adminWhenThe day a proposal or change order is accepted in Portal

  1. In Portal, open the accepted proposal and download its CSV.
  2. In Loadout open Tickets and press New ticket. Keep Import a Portal CSV and press Choose a CSV file, or use Paste the CSV text instead.
  3. Check Preview, first rows as read. If Loadout says "Could not find column headers. Pick them below.", match the columns in the table; Loadout remembers the match for next time.
  4. Read the Skipped rows list. Labor, Fee, and CustomItem rows are skipped on purpose, and a row with a blank quantity is listed with the reason, so nothing disappears without a trace.
  5. Check each line's match. A brand and model Loadout does not know is offered as a new product, or stays a typed line.
  6. Pick the client and the job (New client... and New job... add one), check the Proposal number, pick the Staging bay, and set the Install date so the ticket lands on the right day in Week.
  7. Press Create ticket. Nothing is written before this button.
  8. For a change order, import its CSV again with the same proposal number, or press Add lines on the ticket. Lines are added or adjusted, not duplicated.
  9. For a job with no proposal, choose Build by hand and type the lines.

Recorded: the ticket with client, job, proposal number, where it came from (Imported from CSV, Imported from Portal, or Built by hand), who created it and when, and one line per part grouped by area. No stock moves yet.

SOP-4Pulling and staging

Lay out everything a job needs in its own bay, taking from the shelves first.

WhoWarehouse pulls; Tech may stage and deliverWhenAs soon as the ticket exists, before anything is ordered

  1. Open the ticket and press Pull everything in stock. Loadout fills what it can from each product's usual shelf first and lists what still needs ordering.
  2. Press Show on board. The TV by the bays shows this ticket's lines grouped by the shelf to pull from, in rack order, greys each line as it is scanned into the bay and marks it Staged with a check, and shows On order lines in amber and Short lines in red. Walk the racks in that order.
  3. To pull a line at the shelf: open Scan, scan the shelf label (the location chip changes to that shelf), then scan the product's UPC.
  4. On the product card press Pull for ticket, choose the ticket and line, and press Continue.
  5. In Pull from stock check From and Quantity, tick the serialized units going (or scan their labels), and press Pull. From the desk, the same window opens from Pull from stock on the ticket line.
  6. For a part already on the bench that did not come through a pull, press Stage on the line.
  7. Keep one ticket per bay. If the shelf is short, pull what is there; the rest shows on the line as Short, in red, until a PO covers it, then On order, in amber, with the ETA. Do not borrow from another ticket's bay.
  8. Watch the ticket status: Picking once any line is staged, Staged when every line is laid out.
  9. Each morning, open Week. Every ticket sits on its install day with its lines on the right: Pull, Staged ("Staged 2 of 4" when part of it is in the bay), On order with the ETA, Short, or Delivered. Press a line to open it on the ticket. Work week hides the weekend; past days are faded and today is outlined.

Recorded: a pull movement for each pull with who, product, quantity, the shelf it came from, the bay it went to, and the ticket; a stage movement for Stage.

SOP-5Ordering and tracking

Order what stock cannot cover and know where every ordered part is without opening the PO.

WhoWarehouse or admin, whoever places ordersWhenThe same day as the pull; Tracking every morning after that

  1. On the ticket press Order missing, or Order on a single line. Reorder on Stock, Low stock does the same for shelf stock.
  2. Open Orders and the draft PO. Pick the Vendor; the empty select glows until one is chosen, and a vendor typed there joins the list.
  3. Place the order with the vendor the way you do today.
  4. Press Mark ordered, type the vendor's order number, and pick where it ships: the office, or straight to the job's site.
  5. When the vendor says a line is backordered, set it to Backordered and type the vendor's ETA.
  6. When a ship notice arrives, press Add shipment, paste the tracking number, tick the lines in that box, and type the expected date. Loadout guesses the carrier from the number (1Z is UPS, 12 or 15 digits FedEx, 20 to 22 digits starting with 9 USPS); change it if it guessed wrong. One box can be sent somewhere other than the PO says.
  7. Open Tracking each morning. Packages coming to the office are in the left column, packages going straight to a client's site in the right column grouped by client and job, and the last 30 days of deliveries in Recently delivered along the bottom. Each card shows the carrier, the tracking number as a link, what is in it, the PO and vendor, who it is for, and its status with the date and time. The filters (client and job, vendor, carrier, status, Late only) apply to all three parts.
  8. Late cards read like "Expected Oct 30, 3 days late" in amber; call the vendor about those.
  9. A card that reads Delivered, not received sits at the top of the Office column in amber. That is a box somewhere in the building that nobody scanned: find it and press Receive (SOP-1).
  10. For a box shipped to a job site, press Mark delivered on its card once the site has it, set the time it arrived, and add a note such as who signed. The card reads Delivered to site, and its lines count as received on the PO and staged on the ticket; nothing goes onto the shelves.
  11. Use Note on any card for anything else, for example "left at the front desk".
  12. Carrier status is typed by hand or set from the shipment dates; Loadout does not check UPS or FedEx on its own yet.

Recorded: the PO with vendor, order number, where it ships, each line's unit cost on the day it was ordered, and who marked it ordered and when; each shipment with carrier, tracking number, dates, and a status log with one entry per change (status, time, who, note). Stock changes only at Receive.

SOP-6Wire boxes

Know how many feet are left in every box and which job used each cut, with the name of who cut it.

WhoAnyone who pulls cable: Warehouse or TechWhenWhen the box is received, every time cable is cut, and at the end of every day

  1. Receive the box on its PO (the line asks for a Box label), or without a PO scan its UPC and press Receive a box. The box starts at the start length for its cable type, set in Console, Settings.
  2. Print its label (Print label on the box sheet) and stick it on the side next to the cable exit (SOP-2).
  3. After pulling cable, read the footage printed on the jacket where it leaves the box.
  4. Scan the box's label. The box sheet opens with the box's code, its shelf, and the feet left.
  5. Type that number into Mark now, or tap -50, -100, or -250 to step down from the last mark.
  6. Cut this time fills in (last mark minus mark now). Change it only if you know the real figure.
  7. Check For ticket. It fills in when the box sits in a ticket's bay or you scanned that bay last; pick the ticket if the cable went to another job. When the ticket has a line for this wire, the feet count as staged on it. For job is optional free text.
  8. If the box moved, set Location. Press Save cut.
  9. End of day: before the box goes back on the rack or into the van, scan it and save the mark. It takes thirty seconds and puts the right footage and the right name on the job.
  10. To recount without a cut, tick Recount, no cut; the button changes to Save reading.
  11. When the box runs out, press Empty and confirm Empty the box. Loadout records a final cut to zero and closes the box.

Settled (D12): boxes count down, so the mark is the feet left. Still open (D16): start lengths come from defaults by cable type (1,000 ft for Cat5e, Cat6, and coax; 500 ft for audio, speaker, and stranded); confirm them, or type each open box's real start once.

Recorded: a cut movement with who, the box, the mark before and after, feet cut, and the ticket or job; Save reading writes a reading movement instead. The box's feet left and the product's on hand update.

SOP-8Delivering a ticket and closing it

Mark what left for the site, then settle what was installed and what came back.

WhoTech delivers; Warehouse or admin closesWhenDelivery on install day; close once the install is done

  1. Load the ticket's bay into the van.
  2. Open the ticket and press Deliver to site on each line you are taking today. Each press takes what is staged on that line.
  3. The serialized units on the line check out to you in the same tap; the line's chip reads, for example, "4 of 4 checked out by Tech". The ticket turns Delivered once every line is on site.

Part 3.What Loadout is not

So expectations match what is built.

  • Not a replacement for Portal. Proposals, pricing, and client approvals stay in Portal. Loadout reads the proposal's CSV and hands a status note back.
  • Not accounting software. No invoices and no bills; QuickBooks stays where it is. Loadout's reports are a check against it.
  • No automatic package tracking yet. Carrier status is typed in or comes from the shipment dates. Checking UPS and FedEx on a schedule is a later add-on that would write into the same status log.
  • No barcode until it is scanned once. Portal does not carry UPCs, so the catalog learns each one the first time a box is scanned.
  • No unit-by-unit tracking below the threshold. Cheap stock is counted, not followed, unless someone marks that product Serialize.
  • It records mistakes; it does not block them. Anyone with the right role can change stock. Every change carries a name, so a wrong count can be traced and fixed.

Tech spec

What Loadout runs on, what each piece costs, and a straight answer to the question "will the data still be readable without the app".

In one paragraph

Loadout is a web page, not an app from an app store. It lives on the company's existing work site, opens in any browser, and installs to a phone's home screen like an app. People sign in with their company Google account and nothing else. The records live in a hosted database that the company already pays for and uses for its other internal tools. There is no server of our own to patch or reboot. Beyond the page and the database there are two small moving parts: a service worker on each phone that keeps the app working with no signal, and a Cloudflare worker for the nightly backup and the Zapier import from Portal, which is built and waiting to be deployed.

Will the data stay readable?

The worry is a fair one. A Firestore database can turn into a pile of documents with random names, fields nested five deep, numbers encoded as codes, and the real meaning spread across a dozen places, so that only the app can make sense of it. That is a design choice, not something Firestore forces, and Loadout is built the other way. The rules below are followed by the app and checked before each release.

  • Flat tables with plain names. One collection per kind of thing: products, locations, tickets, orders, shipments, items, wire boxes, movements, people. Each one is the same shape as a spreadsheet tab, and each maps one for one to a table if the data ever moves to SQL.
  • Readable fields, in English. brand, model, qtyNeeded, ftLeft, holderName. No abbreviations, no codes to decode, no field called x1.
  • Names travel with the record. A movement row says "Ubiquiti U6-Pro, from Rack A3 to Bay 2, by Warehouse, for ticket 1042" in words. The ID numbers are there too, but a person can read the row on its own.
  • Readable IDs where the world already has one. A ticket is filed under its Portal proposal number and a purchase order under its PO number. Random IDs are used only where nothing better exists, and the readable fields sit right next to them.
  • No deep nesting and no blobs. At most one level: a ticket has its lines under it, and so does a purchase order. Nothing is stored as an encoded lump.
  • A written data dictionary. Every collection and every field is listed, with its meaning, in the plan and on the About tab in the Console, which reads it from the app's own code so the two cannot drift apart. Someone reading the raw database has the key.
  • A copy outside the database. Export everything gives one CSV per collection (or one workbook) that opens in Excel, and the nightly backup will do the same every day once the worker is live. If Loadout vanished tomorrow, the spreadsheets would still say what is on the shelves and who has what.
  • Dashboards read the same records. The Console's reports are built on the same rows, not on a separate hidden copy, so what accounting sees is what is stored.

Security and access

  • Company Google accounts only. No passwords to manage, no shared logins.
  • Roles are enforced by the database's own rules, not only by hiding buttons: accounting and the wall board physically cannot write, and a tech can only change the fields a scan touches.
  • The movement log cannot be edited or deleted by anyone, including admins. Mistakes are corrected by a new entry with a reason.
  • Photos follow the same roles: admin and warehouse add or remove them, everyone signed in can see them, and each file must be a small image.
  • Everything is served over HTTPS. There is no analytics or tracking script on the page.
  • The page carries a public Firebase key by design; it is locked to the company's site addresses so it works nowhere else.
  • Export everything is the backup today, and the nightly backup takes over once the worker is deployed: a folder in the same Google project the database lives in, listed inside Loadout under Console > Backups. Only admin and accounting can open those files, and nobody can change them from the app. Google keeps the database itself replicated.

Costs

  • Hosting and sign-in: no cost, already in place.
  • Database and photos: within the free tier at this size; a few dollars a month if it grows past it.
  • The worker: Cloudflare's free tier covers one nightly run and the Zapier pushes.
  • Portal automation: only if wanted, and only what Portal charges for its Zapier add-on or Unlimited plan.
  • Hardware: label sheets for an office printer, and a small PC or stick for the TV by the bays.
