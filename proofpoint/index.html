Proofpoint Email Guide

What the Quarantine Digest email is, how to release a legitimate message, and how to use the Proofpoint Essentials portal.

How Proofpoint protects your mailbox

What is Proofpoint?

Proofpoint Essentials is an email security service that sits in front of your mailbox, like a gatekeeper. Every message sent to your address passes through Proofpoint before it reaches your inbox:

  • Clean mail is delivered to your inbox as normal — you never notice Proofpoint was involved.
  • Suspected spam is held in quarantine instead of being delivered. You can review it and release anything legitimate.
  • Viruses and known-malicious mail are blocked outright. For your safety these do not appear in your quarantine and cannot be released by you — if you believe a legitimate message was blocked, contact us and we can check from the admin side.

No filter is perfect. Occasionally a real message lands in quarantine (a “false positive”) — that is what the Quarantine Digest and the portal below are for.

The Quarantine Digest email

OverviewWhat the digest is

The Quarantine Digest is an automatic email from Proofpoint Essentials that lists the messages recently held in your quarantine, so you can rescue anything legitimate with one click — no password needed.

  • It typically arrives once a day. (The exact schedule is set for your organization, so yours may arrive at a different time or more than once a day.)
  • It lists only messages quarantined since your last digest — it is a snapshot, not your full quarantine. The portal always has the complete, up-to-the-minute list.
  • No new spam, no digest. If nothing was quarantined, no email is sent that cycle — a quiet day is normal.

The digest comes from Proofpoint Essentials (us2.proofpointessentials.com), not from a person. The exact sender address can vary slightly.
The first row is a real message caught by mistake — a payroll statement you were expecting. That is the kind of message you release.
The other rows are correctly caught spam. Leave them alone — you never have to do anything with spam; it deletes itself.
Release delivers that one message. Release and Approve also adds the sender to your safe list so future mail skips the spam filter.

ActionsWhat the digest buttons do

  • Release — delivers that one message to your inbox. Future messages from the sender are still filtered normally. Use this for one-off mail, or when you are not sure you want everything the sender might send later.
  • Release and Approve — delivers the message and adds the sender to your safe senders list, so their future mail comes straight to your inbox. Use this for senders you trust and expect to hear from again.

A released message usually appears in your inbox within a few minutes. If you do not see it, check your Spam (Gmail) or Junk Email (Outlook) folder — your mail provider runs its own filter after Proofpoint and can occasionally move a released message there.

Only release messages you were actually expecting. If a quarantined message looks suspicious or too good to be true, leave it alone — sitting in quarantine it cannot hurt anything, and it is deleted automatically after about 30 days. Not sure? Ask us before releasing.

The Proofpoint Essentials portal

Sign inLogging in to the portal

The digest covers day-to-day needs, but the portal gives you the full picture: every quarantined message, your safe and blocked sender lists, and the Emergency Inbox.

  1. Go to us2.proofpointessentials.com.
  2. Sign in with your full work email address.
  3. Use the password Proofpoint sent you when your account was created — or click Forgot password? to set a new one. (This is a separate password from your email password.)

QuarantineReviewing quarantined mail in the portal

Once signed in, open your quarantine (under Logs, filtered to quarantined messages). From there you can:

  • See the complete list of everything currently held — not just what the last digest showed.
  • Preview a message safely before deciding — viewing it in the portal does not run attachments or load anything on your computer.
  • Release or Release and Approve one or more messages: tick the checkboxes, then use the action buttons.

Quarantined messages are kept for about 30 days and then deleted automatically — you never need to clean up your quarantine.

Sender listsSafe and blocked senders

The portal also holds your personal sender lists:

  • Safe senders — mail from these addresses skips the spam filter. Senders you “Release and Approve” end up here; you can also add or remove addresses directly.
  • Blocked senders — mail from these addresses is always quarantined or discarded. Useful for a persistent nuisance sender that keeps slipping through.

Added someone by mistake? Open the list and remove the entry — the change takes effect for future mail.

Safe-listing does not bypass virus scanning — a safe-listed sender whose account is hacked will still have malicious attachments blocked.

Emergency InboxIf your email is ever down

Because Proofpoint sits in front of your mailbox, it keeps receiving your mail even if your email service (Google Workspace or Microsoft 365) has an outage. During an outage you can sign in to the portal and use the Emergency Inbox to read and reply to new mail. When your mail service comes back, everything received during the outage is delivered to your normal inbox automatically.

Frequently asked questions

An email I'm expecting never arrived. What do I check?
  1. Check your Spam / Junk folder in Gmail or Outlook first — your mail provider's own filter runs after Proofpoint.
  2. Check your most recent Quarantine Digest email.
  3. Sign in to the portal and check the full quarantine list — the digest only shows recent items.
  4. Still nothing? Contact us. The message may have been blocked at a level you cannot see (for example as a virus), or it may never have been sent — we can trace it from the admin side.
I released a message but it's not in my inbox.

Check your Spam (Gmail) or Junk Email (Outlook) folder. Releasing a message gets it past Proofpoint, but your mail provider still runs its own junk filter and can move the message there. Mark it as “Not spam” / “Not junk” to help future delivery.

I haven't received a digest in a while. Is something broken?

Probably not — the digest is only sent when there are new quarantined messages since the last one. No new spam means no digest. You can confirm everything is working by signing in to the portal and looking at your quarantine directly. If you believe digests have genuinely stopped (for example, the portal shows new quarantined mail but no digest arrived), contact us and we will check your digest settings.

Can I change how often I get the digest, or turn it off?

The digest schedule is managed centrally for your organization. If you would like it more often, less often, or off entirely for your account, just ask — it is a quick change on our side.

How do I unblock a sender I blocked by mistake?

Sign in to the portal, open your blocked senders list, and remove the entry. The same works in reverse for removing someone from your safe senders list. If you cannot find the list, contact us and we can fix it from the admin side.

How long do messages stay in quarantine?

About 30 days, then they are deleted automatically. If you might need a quarantined message, release it before then — after deletion it cannot be recovered.

Is it safe to just ignore the digest?

Yes. Nothing in quarantine requires action — spam deletes itself after about 30 days. The only cost of ignoring the digest is that a legitimate message caught by mistake would sit unnoticed. A quick skim of each digest (10 seconds — scan the sender names for anyone you know) is all we recommend.

Can I file the digests into their own folder automatically?

Yes — create an inbox rule that matches the digest and moves it to a folder:

  • Gmail: open a digest, click the three-dot menu, pick Filter messages like this, then Create filter and choose Apply the label (for example a “Quarantine” label).
  • Outlook (web): right-click a digest, pick Advanced actions → Create rule, and choose a folder to move it to.

If you do this, remember to actually glance at that folder now and then — out of sight is how false positives get missed.
