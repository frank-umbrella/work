Storyboard and functional SOP
Draft v0.1

Spool

How product moves through the shop, from approved proposal to installed, and what gets recorded at each step

Draft for review, 2026-10-26. Nothing is built yet; this page is the thing to mark up.

Read this first

Spool is a web app for the office and the warehouse, built to be used on a phone in the rack room and on a desktop at the front. It turns an approved Portal proposal into a pick ticket and follows every part on it from ordered to installed. It keeps a running count of what is on the shelves, including how many feet are left in each wire box. Every change is written to a log with the name of the person who made it, so any count can be explained later.

Every client, name, number, price, and address on this page is invented sample data. The buttons drawn inside the pictures are pictures; the only things you can click on this page are the contents links below and the print button at the bottom.

Part 1.A day in the warehouse

One job, start to finish: Acme Dental's main office, pick ticket #1042. Phone screens are what a person holds in the warehouse or the van; desktop screens are the office.

1Approved proposal arrives

What you doDownload the accepted proposal's CSV from Portal, then in Spool choose New ticket and Import CSV. Check the client, job, and proposal number, and press Create ticket.

What Spool recordsNo movement yet: pick ticket #1042 with client, job, proposal 2291, who created it and when, and every part grouped by area.

2Pull from stock

What you doScan the Rack A3 shelf label, scan the U6-Pro, confirm 2 for ticket #1042, and set them in Bay 2. The line turns green and shows 2 of 4 staged.

What Spool recordsA pick movement: who, U6-Pro x2, from Rack A3, into Bay 2, for ticket #1042.

3Order the rest

What you doOn the ticket, press Order missing. The three lines that stock cannot fill go onto a draft PO; when the order is placed with ADI, type the order number and press Mark ordered.

What Spool recordsNo stock moves: PO 7731 to ADI, each line tied to its ticket line, and who marked it ordered and when.

4Track it

What you doOpen Tracking each morning. Receive the switch FedEx says was delivered yesterday, and call ADI about the UDM-SE that is two days late.

What Spool recordsNothing until you act: the page reads PO lines and shipments (carrier, tracking number, expected date) and puts delivered-but-not-received on top.

5Receive

What you doOpen PO 7731 in Receive mode and scan each box's UPC; the count on each line ticks along. The UDM-SE costs at least $250, so Spool asks for an item sticker, and you print it and stick it on before the box leaves the bench.

What Spool recordsA receive movement per box: who, product, quantity, PO 7731 and its shipment, into Bay 2 for #1042, plus a new item record for the UDM-SE.

6Label

What you doIn More, Labels, pick the bay, the wire box, and the AP, and print one sheet. Stick each one where it will be scanned: on the bay upright, on the box beside the cable exit, on the AP itself.

What Spool recordsNo movement: each sticker carries the record's id and a readable line, so a torn one can still be typed in.

7Wire box

What you doAfter pulling cable, scan the box's QR, type the footage mark where the cable leaves the box (640), and press Save. Spool works out the cut: 360 ft.

What Spool recordsA cut movement: who, Cat6 blue box 3, from mark 1000 to 640, 360 ft, for ticket #1042.

8Load the van

What you doWith Bay 2 loaded into the van, open #1042 and press Deliver to site. The six serialized units on the ticket check out to you in that one tap.

What Spool recordsA deliver movement per line and a checkout movement per item: who, item, holder (you), job, ticket #1042, time.

9On site, years later

What you doOn a service call, scan the sticker on the rack with your phone, or type the code under it into Items at the office. The page shows the unit went in on 10/31/2026 under proposal 2291.

What Spool recordsNothing new: the item's history already holds the receive, stage, checkout, and install movements, each with a name and a date.

10Something comes back

What you doBack at the shop with an AP that was not needed, scan its sticker, press Check in, confirm it goes to Rack A3, and pick its condition.

What Spool recordsA checkin movement: who, item 4F2A9D, back from ticket #1042, into Rack A3, condition Good.

11End of day

What you doBefore a wire box goes back on the rack or into the van, scan it and enter the mark. Then check that the pill at the top says Synced, with nothing pending.

What Spool recordsA cut movement per box (who, box, marks, feet, ticket); Synced means every change from the day reached the server.

12Accounting

What you doOpen Console, Reports, pick Delivered per job and a date range, and press Export XLSX. The same page has spend by vendor, items out, and wire used per job and per person.

What Spool recordsNothing: reports only read the movement log, and the accounting role has no edit buttons anywhere, so a count cannot change from here.

Part 2.Functional SOP

Eleven procedures, in the order product moves. Button names match the screens above. "Recorded" is what lands in the movement log, which is the audit trail behind every count.

SOP-1Receiving product

Get every box that arrives into Spool the day it lands, against its PO when there is one.

WhoWarehouseWhenThe day a delivery arrives, before the box is opened or shelved

With a PO

  1. Open Tracking and press Receive on the row, or open Orders and find the PO number on the packing slip.
  2. Press Receive on the shipment. The phone camera opens in Receive mode.
  3. Scan the UPC on each box. Each scan counts one unit against the matching PO line.
  4. If Spool asks which line a UPC belongs to, pick the line. Spool saves that UPC to the product, so it is known next time.
  5. When a unit costs at or above the QR threshold in Settings (default $250.00), Spool makes an item record and shows Print item sticker. Print it and stick it on the unit before it leaves the bench.
  6. Set the box where Spool says: the ticket's staging bay if the line serves a ticket, otherwise its shelf. Scan the bay or shelf label to confirm.
  7. If the shipment is short, leave the line open. It stays on Tracking until the rest arrives.

Without a PO

  1. On the Scan tab, scan the box's UPC and press Receive.
  2. Type the quantity and scan the shelf label it is going on.
  3. To follow a cheaper product unit by unit anyway (for example every AP), open the product and press Serialize; from then on every unit gets an item sticker at receive.

Still open (D11): $250 is the plan's default; the actual threshold, or "everything with a serial number", is yours to set.

Recorded: a receive movement with who, product, quantity, PO and shipment, the bay or shelf it went to, and the ticket when the line serves one; plus one item record per unit at or above the threshold.

SOP-2Labeling

Put a QR sticker on every place and every followed thing, so one scan answers where it is and who has it.

WhoWarehouseWhenA new shelf, bay, or van is set up; a wire box or serialized item is received; a sticker is torn or will not scan

  1. Open More, then Labels.
  2. Pick what to print: locations (shelves, bays, vans), wire boxes, or items.
  3. Pick the sheet: the 2x4 sheet (10 per sheet) for bays and anything that lives in a rack, the small sheet (30 per sheet) for shelves and handhelds.
  4. Press Print sheet and print on the label stock.
  5. Stick bay labels on the bay upright at eye height, shelf labels on the shelf edge, wire box labels on the side next to the cable exit, and item labels on the unit itself, not only the carton, so the sticker goes into the rack with it.
  6. Scan each new sticker once to check it reads.
  7. To re-label, type the readable line under the old sticker into the field below the camera, open the record, press Print label, and stick the new one over the old one.

Recorded: no movement. The sticker carries the record's id and a readable line, so a torn sticker can always be typed in.

SOP-3Building a pick ticket from an approved proposal

Turn an accepted Portal proposal into the one working document for that job.

WhoWarehouse or adminWhenThe day a proposal or change order is accepted in Portal

  1. In Portal, open the accepted proposal and download its CSV.
  2. In Spool, go to Tickets, press New ticket, then Import CSV. Drop the file in or paste its text.
  3. Check the preview. If Spool says "Could not find column headers", pick them in the mapping table; Spool remembers the mapping for next time.
  4. Read the Skipped rows list. Labor, fees, and custom items are skipped on purpose; a row with a blank quantity is listed with the reason.
  5. Leave Create product ticked for parts that are not in the catalog yet.
  6. Pick or create the client and the job, and check the proposal number.
  7. Pick the staging bay now if you know it.
  8. Press Create ticket. Nothing is created before this button.
  9. For a change order, import its CSV onto the existing ticket. Lines are added or adjusted, not duplicated.

Recorded: the ticket with client, job, proposal number, who created it and when, and one line per part grouped by area. No stock moves yet.

SOP-4Picking and staging

Lay out everything a job needs in its own bay, taking from the shelves first.

WhoWarehouseWhenAs soon as the ticket exists, before anything is ordered

  1. Open the ticket and press Pull everything we have in stock. Spool fills what it can from the shelves and lists what still has to be ordered.
  2. At the shelf, open the Scan tab and scan the shelf label. The location chip changes to that shelf.
  3. Scan the product's UPC, or its item sticker if it has one.
  4. Press Pick for ticket, pick the ticket, and confirm the count.
  5. Carry it to the ticket's bay and scan the bay label. The line's staged count goes up.
  6. Keep one ticket per bay. Nothing goes into a bay or comes out of it without a scan.
  7. If the shelf is short, pick what is there. The rest stays as To order on the line; do not borrow from another ticket's bay.
  8. Watch the ticket status: it shows Picking once any line is staged and Staged when every line is laid out.

Recorded: a pick movement for each pull with who, product, quantity, the shelf it came from, the bay it went to, and the ticket.

SOP-5Ordering and tracking

Order what stock cannot cover and know where every ordered part is without opening the PO.

WhoWarehouse or admin, whoever places ordersWhenThe same day as the pick; Tracking every morning after that

  1. On the ticket, press Order missing, or Order on a single line. Reorder on the low-stock list does the same for shelf stock.
  2. Open Orders and the draft PO. Pick the vendor; Mark ordered stays off until a vendor is picked.
  3. Place the order with the vendor the way you do today.
  4. Type the vendor's order number and press Mark ordered.
  5. When the vendor says a line is backordered, set it to Backordered and type the ETA.
  6. When a ship notice arrives, press Add shipment, tick the lines in that box, and paste the tracking number. Spool guesses the carrier from the number; pick it yourself if it cannot tell.
  7. Open Tracking each morning. Late rows read like "Expected 10/24, 2 days late"; call the vendor about those.
  8. Rows that are delivered but not received sit at the top in amber. That is a box somewhere in the building that nobody scanned: find it and receive it (SOP-1).
  9. In this first version, carrier status is typed by hand or set from the shipment dates; Spool does not check UPS or FedEx on its own.

Recorded: the PO with vendor, order number, who marked it ordered and when, and the ticket line each PO line serves; each shipment with carrier, tracking number, and dates. Stock changes only when the shipment is received.

SOP-6Wire boxes

Know how many feet are left in every box and which job used each cut, with the name of who cut it.

WhoAnyone who pulls cable: Warehouse or TechWhenWhen the box is received, every time cable is cut, and at the end of every day

  1. Receive the box by scanning the UPC printed on it. Spool makes a box record starting at 1000 ft (change it if the box says otherwise) and names it, for example Cat6 blue box 3.
  2. Print its sticker and put it on the side next to the cable exit (SOP-2).
  3. After pulling cable, read the footage printed on the jacket where it leaves the box.
  4. Scan the box's QR. The box sheet opens.
  5. Type that number into Mark now. Spool fills in Cut this time (last mark minus mark now); change it only if you know the real figure.
  6. Check the For ticket chip. Spool fills it from the bay the box sits in; change it if the cable went to another job.
  7. Press Save.
  8. End of day: before the box goes back on the rack or into the van, scan it and enter the mark. It takes thirty seconds and puts the right footage and the right name on the job.
  9. To recount without a cut, use the same sheet with Cut set to 0 and the note "recount".
  10. When the box runs out, press Empty. Spool records a final cut to zero and closes the box.

Still open (D12): the plan assumes your boxes print footage counting down from 1000, so the mark is the feet left; if yours count up from 0, the math on the box sheet flips.

Recorded: a cut movement with who, the box, the mark before and after, feet cut, and the ticket; a recount writes a reading movement instead. The box's feet left and the product's on-hand update.

SOP-7Check-out and check-in of serialized items

Know who has every stickered unit, for which job, and what shape it came back in.

WhoTech checks out and in; Warehouse also moves items, changes the holder, and clears returnsWhenEvery time a stickered unit leaves the shop or comes back

Check out

  1. Scan the item sticker. The item card shows where it is and who has it.
  2. Press Check out and pick the job, or Van stock if it is riding in your van with no job yet.
  3. Add a note if it helps, and save. You are the holder.

Check in

  1. Back at the shop, scan the item sticker and press Check in.
  2. Confirm where it goes. Spool suggests its usual shelf, or the bay of the ticket it was out for.
  3. Pick the condition: Good, Damaged, or Missing parts. Say what is wrong in the note.
  4. A unit that comes back from a client's home goes in as Returned, not stock. Warehouse looks it over and moves it to In stock.
  5. If someone else has a unit that shows under your name, ask Warehouse to change the holder; do not check it in for them.
  6. On Fridays, a manager opens Items, Out, to see who has what and since when. Nudge copies a one-line reminder to paste into a message.

Recorded: a checkout or checkin movement with who, the item, the holder, the job, the time, where it went back to, and its condition.

SOP-8Delivering a ticket and closing it

Mark what left for the site, then settle what was installed and what came back.

WhoTech delivers; Warehouse or admin closesWhenDelivery on install day; close once the install is done

  1. Load the ticket's bay into the van.
  2. Open the ticket and press Deliver to site (on every line, or on the lines you are taking today).
  3. The stickered units on those lines check out to you for this job in the same tap.
  4. Press Copy for Portal and paste the status into the proposal (SOP-11).
  5. After the install, check in anything that came back unused (SOP-7) before closing.
  6. Open the ticket and press Close.
  7. For each leftover, Spool asks: Return to stock (scan it onto a shelf) or Write off (type the reason).
  8. Stickered units still out for this job become Installed with the job and proposal. They stop counting as stock and keep their history.

Settled: units become Installed at close, not at delivery, so a unit that comes back unused never shows as installed.

Recorded: a deliver movement per line (who, product, quantity, ticket, job), a checkout per stickered unit, and on close a return or write-off movement for each leftover with its reason.

SOP-9Counts and adjustments

Fix a count that is wrong, and leave a reason anyone can read later.

WhoWarehouse and admin only; techs and accounting cannot adjustWhenWhenever a shelf count does not match Spool

  1. Before changing anything, open the product and read its recent movements. A gap is often an unscanned pick or receive; record that movement instead of adjusting.
  2. Count what is on the shelf.
  3. On the Scan tab, scan the shelf label, then the product, and press Count. From a desk, open the product and press Adjust.
  4. Type the number you counted.
  5. If it differs from Spool, type the reason. It is required: damaged, found, lost, miscounted, or used without a ticket.
  6. Save. On-hand changes and the reason shows in the log.
  7. For wire, do not adjust. Take a reading on the box sheet (SOP-6).

Recorded: an adjust movement with who, product, location, the count before and after, and the reason.

SOP-10Roles and the Console

Give each person the access their job needs, and let accounting pull numbers without touching stock.

WhoAdmin for people and settings; Accounting for reportsWhenA new hire, a role change, someone leaving, or month end

Admin

Everything, plus People and Settings: QR threshold, show costs, carriers, label size, default wire box length.

Warehouse

Receive, pick, stage, order, adjust counts, print labels, move and retire items, change a holder.

Tech

Scan, pick, check out and check in, and enter wire marks. No product edits, no adjustments.

Accounting

Reads everything and the Console reports. Sees no edit buttons at all.

  1. Admin: the new person signs in once with their company Google account. Only company accounts get in.
  2. Open Console, then People. Find the newcomer, pick a role, and press Confirm.
  3. To change a role, pick the new one. When someone leaves, press Deactivate; their name stays on everything they did.
  4. Accounting: open Console, then Reports.
  5. Pick the report: stock on hand and value, spend by vendor, delivered per job, items out, wire usage, movements, or aging.
  6. Set the date range and press Export CSV or Export XLSX.
  7. If a number looks wrong, tell Warehouse; accounting cannot change a count.

Still open (D2): who is in each role, and whether techs may receive product or only Warehouse.

Recorded: People keeps each person's role and whether they are confirmed. Reports only read the movement log; exporting changes nothing.

SOP-11Portal notes

Keep the proposal in Portal up to date on where its product stands.

WhoWhoever moves the ticket: Warehouse or adminWhenWhen a ticket is staged, ordered, delivered, or closed

  1. Open the ticket in Spool.
  2. Press Copy for Portal. Spool copies a status block like the one below.
  3. In Portal, open the proposal and its internal notes.
  4. Paste the block at the end. Do not replace the notes already there.
  5. Save in Portal.
[Spool 2026-10-23 14:05] Pick ticket #1042 - Acme Dental, Main office
Staged 9/12 lines in Bay 2. To order: 3 lines (PO 7731 to ADI, ordered 10/21,
UPS 1Z999AA10123456784 shipped 10/23). Backordered: Ubiquiti U6-Pro x2.

Still open (D1): on Portal's top plan Spool could add this note by itself; until we know which plan Umbrella is on, copy and paste is the way.

Recorded: nothing changes in Spool. Portal keeps the pasted note on the proposal.

Part 3.What Spool is not

So expectations match what gets built.

  • Not a replacement for Portal. Proposals, pricing, and client approvals stay in Portal. Spool reads the proposal's CSV and hands a status note back.
  • Not accounting software. No invoices and no bills; QuickBooks stays where it is. Spool's reports are a check against it.
  • No automatic package tracking in the first version. Carrier status is typed in or comes from the shipment dates. Checking UPS and FedEx on a schedule is a later add-on.
  • No barcode until it is scanned once. Portal does not carry UPCs, so the catalog learns each one the first time a box is scanned.
  • No unit-by-unit tracking below the threshold. Cheap stock is counted, not followed, unless someone marks that product Serialize.
  • It records mistakes; it does not block them. Anyone with the right role can change stock. Every change carries a name, so a wrong count can be traced and fixed.
